## AI source check (CHR-20, 2026-11-16)

Tracked in Linear as CHR-20. Claude Opus 5.5, an AI model by Anthropic, checked every citation, quotation, date,
name and figure on the page and its maps against a source it could read in full, and cut back or removed what those
sources did not support. Every "not yet checked" label, and the three review flags, were removed. This is still an AI
check, not a review by a person; the specialist read-through is CHR-21.

There are **no B rows**: the issue's book list ends around AD 600, and no list for later eras has been chosen.
Statements about what historians think were cut back to what the sources show, with Schaff's and the Catholic
Encyclopedia's judgments given by name. Nothing on this page waits on the owner's library check. The third flag, the
era's length and end date "to be confirmed by the owner", is a planning question, not a claim on the page; the plan
records the end at 1789 as decided, and the flag was dropped.

Project Gutenberg was down for maintenance during the check, and several other text sites are not reachable from the
checking environment. Hume's Dialogues could be read only as far as the Wikisource title page and editor's note, and no
public-domain text of Kant's 1784 essay or of Spener's Pia Desideria was available.

### What changed on the page

- **Corrected:**
  - Jansen "the Flemish bishop" became the bishop of Ypres; Schaff and the encyclopedia say he was born near Leerdam in
    Holland.
  - "Spain and its colonies expelled the Jesuits, the first step in a wave of suppressions" was wrong: Portugal (1759)
    and France (1764) came first. The event is now 1759 to 1767.
  - Edwards's sermon "preached in Enfield, Connecticut" became "at Enfield": the source does not name the colony.
  - Joseph II's edict granted toleration to "Protestants and Orthodox Christians": Schaff names the Augsburg and Helvetic
    Confessions only, and the Orthodox were removed. "Reduced the power of the Catholic Church over monasteries and
    education" was removed; no source read gives it (the encyclopedia has no article on Josephinism).
  - Joseph II's reign dates were removed; no source read gives them.
  - The Holy Synod "run by a state official" became the encyclopedia's statement, with its name, that the lay procurator
    was its real head.
  - "The Estates-General met at Versailles" became the encyclopedia's account: the Assembly sat at Versailles until the
    king was brought to Paris on 6 October 1789. "Church lands were taken into state control later that year" became the decree of 2 November 1789.
  - The Civil Constitution "made priests into paid civil servants" became what the encyclopedia lists: election, salaries
    and the oath.
- **Removed:**
  - Kant's essay "What is Enlightenment?" (1784): no source read gives it. The event is replaced by the Critique of Pure
    Reason of 1781, from the encyclopedia.
  - Hume "questioned miracles and the argument from design": the Dialogues could not be read beyond the title page. His
    entry gives only his dates, the 1779 publication, and the encyclopedia's account of his effect on Kant.
  - "Pia Desideria is often treated as the start of Pietism" became the encyclopedia's dating of the movement to about
    1670.
- **Added from the sources:** Cum occasione (1653); Port-Royal (1709 to 1713); Francke and Halle; Herrnhut and the
  Moravians (1722); Louis XVI's edict (1787); the restoration of the Jesuits (1814); Clement XIV's election; St
  Petersburg; the clergy in the Assembly of 1789.
- **Figures:** "shows the calm devotion that the Catholic renewal encouraged" and "Pictures like this were made for
  private prayer and for the popular devotion of the period" were removed. The Peter the Great piece is a plaque, as the
  museum record says, not a bust. The Good Counsel caption now rests on the encyclopedia.
- **Debates:** the three "historians debate ... The page does not yet give a view" sections became what Schaff and the
  encyclopedia say, attributed. A section on Pascal and the Jesuits gives both sources' views.
- **Worship:** "Their effect on parish life is debated" and "processions and pilgrimages ... encouraged by bishops and
  religious orders" were removed.
- **Glossary:** "Concordat", "Enlightenment" and "Revelation", no longer used on the page, were replaced by terms the page
  uses.

### What changed on the maps

Both maps draw no borders, so the intros' "Catholic, Lutheran, Reformed and Orthodox lands ... with the Habsburg and
Ottoman frontiers", "the Habsburg and Prussian courts" and "Boundaries are simplified" were replaced by lists of the
places named on the page, and a credit was added to each.

- **Confessions about 1700 (the overview):** Edinburgh, Madrid, Lisbon, Amsterdam, Vienna and Stockholm, not named on
  the page, were removed. Ypres and Frankfurt were added.
- **Europe in 1789:** Edinburgh, London, Madrid, Halle, Herrnhut, Vienna, St Petersburg and Stockholm, not named in the
  1789 map's subject, were removed. Königsberg was added. Lisbon stays: the page now names the patriarchate of Lisbon.
- **Herrnhut** was moved 15.0 km east, to the town.
