### What changed on the page

- **Corrected or made exact:**
  - Worms: Luther's reply is now quoted from Schaff, with his note that "Here I stand" may not be Luther's exact words.
  - The Peasants' War: Frankenhausen and Schaff's estimate added; "drew on the language of reform" removed.
  - The Schmalkaldic War "1546 to 1547" became 1547, the date of Mühlberg, the only date the sources read give.
  - Henry VIII "1509 to 1547" became "from 1509": the encyclopedia gives his death as 28 January 1546, and no source
    read gives 1547.
  - Elizabeth I: "Her Acts of Supremacy and Uniformity (1559)" became the Thirty-nine Articles of 1563, which the
    sources read do give; her end date was removed for the same reason.
  - Charles V "ruled over Spain, the Netherlands and much of Italy" became what Schaff says.
  - St Bartholomew's Day: "Thousands of Huguenots" and "the number of deaths is debated" became Schaff's "very many
    Protestants".
  - Edict of Nantes: "limited toleration" became Schaff's "a nearly common footing".
  - Westphalia: the peace was signed at Münster on 24 October 1648, after negotiations at Münster and Osnabrück.
  - Melanchthon's Loci "the first Protestant systematic theology" became Schaff's "marks an epoch".
- **Added:** the confession of Cyril Lucaris (1629), the East's only entry, which the plan had asked for; Luther's
  German Bible; the Zurich Bible; the Pilgrimage of Grace; Prague 1618, Magdeburg and Lützen.
- **Figures:** "which helped his face become known across Germany" (Luther) and "Prints like these were sold to
  ordinary buyers for private devotion, and they were copied widely" (Dürer) were removed. The Melanchthon caption now
  rests on Schaff.
- **Debates:** "Historians debate ... The page does not yet give a view" became what the sources show.
