## AI source check (CHR-20, 2026-11-16)

Tracked in Linear as CHR-20. Claude Opus 5.5, an AI model by Anthropic, checked every citation, quotation, date,
name and figure on the page and its maps against a source it could read in full, and cut back or removed what those
sources did not support. Every "not yet checked" and "(unchecked)" label, and the four review flags, were removed. This
is still an AI check, not a review by a person; the specialist read-through is CHR-21.

There are **no B rows**: the issue's book list ends around AD 600, and no list for later eras has been chosen.
Statements about what historians think were cut back to what the sources show, with Schaff's and the Catholic
Encyclopedia's judgments given by name. Nothing on this page waits on the owner's library check.

Two flags were planning notes, not claims on the page, and were dropped from the page but are kept below for the owner:
the era's length and 1870 end date "to be confirmed by the owner", and the need for an Eastern specialist.

Project Gutenberg was down for maintenance during the check, and several other text sites (New Advent, Wikipedia,
the Newman Reader, Project Canterbury) are not reachable from the checking environment. The Wikisource API was
rate-limiting, so Catholic Encyclopedia articles were read as raw wikitext from Wikisource. Newman's Apologia (CCEL has
only page images), Chateaubriand's Génie du christianisme, and Lamennais's writings could not be read.

### What changed on the page

- **Corrected:**
  - The 1870 vote: the two sources disagree. Schaff counts 535 present and 533 placet; the encyclopedia counts 435
    assembled and 433 placet. The page gives both.
  - Gregory V was hanged "in April": no source read gives the month, and it was removed.
  - Bulgaria: the firman date (11 March 1870) and the patriarch's refusals were added; the excommunication is placed
    after the first exarch's election in 1872, as the encyclopedia tells it.
  - Keble "Oxford professor of poetry" was removed: no source read gives it. He is now a fellow of Oriel, as the
    encyclopedia says.
  - "The restored governments were often suspicious of the church" and "The movement changed Anglicanism for good" were
    removed: no source read says so.
  - The Concordat "gave the state a say in the appointment of bishops" became the encyclopedia's terms: the government
    presents and the pope institutes.
  - The Holy Alliance "of the Christian powers" became the three monarchs named in the encyclopedia, with the
    encyclopedia's account of the papacy's coldness towards it. A first draft of this check gave the three rulers'
    confessions; the Prussian royal house was Reformed, not Lutheran, and no source read states them, so they were taken out.
  - "Pius IX ... then turned against the liberal revolutions of 1848" became the encyclopedia's account of the flight
    and return.
  - Schaff's note in vol. 2 dates the reading of Ineffabilis Deus to "Dec. 8, 1864"; his own § 28 and the encyclopedia
    give 8 December 1854, which the page uses.
- **Removed:**
  - The Gülhane edict of 1839 and the Tanzimat: no source read gives them.
  - The Second Great Awakening and camp meetings, missionary societies, Kierkegaard, and biblical criticism: no source
    read gives them.
  - "Evangelical revival spread through England, Scotland and America": replaced by events Schaff gives (the Prussian
    Union, the Disciples of Christ, the Evangelical Alliance).
  - "His execution is one of the best known events of the war": no source read says so.
- **Added from the sources:** the rejection of the Civil Constitution (1791); the Supreme Being (1794); Tolentino and
  the Roman Republic (1797 to 1798); the German secularization (1803); Savona and Fontainebleau; the Prussian Union;
  the Disciples of Christ; Lamennais and Mirari vos; the Church of Greece (1833 and 1850); the Evangelical Alliance;
  Castelfidardo (1860); the texts of Ineffabilis Deus, Syllabus 80 and Pastor Aeternus.
- **Debates:** the three "historians debate ... The page does not yet give a view" sections became what Schaff and the
  encyclopedia say, attributed. A new section gives the encyclopedia's account of the Holy Alliance.
- **Glossary:** "Infallibility" (worded beyond the decree) and "Tanzimat" (removed from the page) were replaced by
  terms the page uses, with "Ex cathedra" in the decree's own words.
- **Further reading:** the placeholder "A study of the Oxford Movement (to choose)" became Chadwick's *The Spirit of the
  Oxford Movement* (1990), and Chadwick's *A History of the Popes, 1830–1914* (1998) was added.

### What changed on the maps

The maps draw no borders, so "Boundaries are simplified" and "simplified and approximate" were replaced by lists of
the places named on the page, and a credit was added to each.

- **Europe (the overview):** retitled "Europe, 1789 to 1870". Madrid, Lisbon, Berlin, St Petersburg, Athens and
  Edinburgh, not named on the page, were removed. Oxford, Fontainebleau, Valence, Savona and Venice were added.
- **The Papal States:** Naples, Milan and Ravenna, not named, were removed. Ferrara, Tolentino, Castelfidardo, Gaeta and
  Savona were added. "Lost in 1790" (Avignon) and "occupied in 1809 and again in 1860 and 1870" were replaced by what the
  page says.
- **The Balkans:** retitled "The Balkans and Constantinople, 1821 to 1870". Athens, Nafplio, Belgrade, Bucharest, Sofia,
  Plovdiv, Thessalonica, Varna and Smyrna, not named, were removed. Tirnovo and Ochrida, the old Bulgarian patriarchates,
  were added with the sentence that names them.

### For the owner

- **Era boundary:** the plan lists the 1870 end date and the 81-year length as decisions "to confirm". The review flag
  was dropped from the page because it is not a claim, but the decision is still open.
- **The East:** the page's Eastern material rests only on the Catholic Encyclopedia, a Western source. An Eastern
  specialist (CHR-21), or an Orthodox source in the public domain, would let the page give the Greek and Bulgarian
  churches' own accounts, and restore the Tanzimat if a source is found.
- **Protestant revival:** the Second Great Awakening, missionary societies, Kierkegaard and biblical criticism were
  removed for want of a readable source. A public-domain history of nineteenth-century Protestantism would let them
  return.
- **Sources not read:** Newman's Apologia (CCEL has only page images; Project Gutenberg was down), Chateaubriand's
  Génie du christianisme, and Lamennais's writings.
