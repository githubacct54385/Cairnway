# Plan: The Fifteenth Era (AD 1989 to the present)

> Drafted 2026-11-12 as the planning document for the fifteenth era, after the fourteenth era (AD 1945 to 1989). Planning
> only. Every date, figure and book detail is from the drafter's general knowledge and is **U** (unverified) until checked,
> unless marked **F** (read in the full text of a named public-domain or freely licensed edition). Current statistics must
> be taken from the latest published survey or report, with its date recorded.

Proposed slug: `the-church-today` (file `src/content/eras/the-church-today.md`, `order: 15`). Title is a suggestion.

## Why this boundary

The fourteenth era ends in 1989, the fall of the Berlin Wall, and this era begins there. It covers the churches from the end
of the Cold War to the present day. Unlike the earlier eras, this one is still happening, so the page must say what it
knows, when it knew it, and what the sources do not yet settle. Every figure needs a date.

## Decisions (owner, to confirm)

1. **Start: AD 1989.** Follows the fourteenth era.
2. **End: the present.** The page is dated "as of" the latest source checked, with the date stated at the top. The
   schema needs a numeric `endYear`, so the proposal is `endYear: 2026` (the year of drafting), updated when the page is
   revised. To confirm.
3. **Form: a fifteenth era, not a closing essay.** It keeps the site's timeline and source rules. The alternative, a
   separate closing page, is not recommended, because it would be the only page without a timeline entry or a source table.
4. **Balance and the no-verdict rule.** The page sets out disputed matters (sexuality and ordination, abuse and its
   handling, the Ukraine war and the Orthodox churches, persecution counts) and gives no verdict the sources do not support,
   as the thirteenth era does with the Holocaust section. The frontier-model review must check this closely.
5. **Living people and organisations.** The page does not judge living figures. It describes what they have said and done
   and cites it. Statements by current church leaders are quoted only in short passages within licence.
6. **Scope: the world church.** Africa, Asia and Latin America get as much room as Europe and North America, because most
   Christians now live there.

## Structure of the page

1. **The shape of the church today (1989 to 2026):** where Christians live now, and how that differs from 1989. Figures for
   membership by region, with dates and sources. The growth of the global South and the decline in Europe.
2. **The end of the Cold War and the churches of the East (1989 to 2026):** the post-communist churches, the Russian
   Orthodox Church's position after 1991, and the Ukrainian church's autocephaly (2018) and the war from 2022.
3. **Divisions over sexuality and ordination (1989 to 2026):** the Anglican Communion (the Lambeth Conference resolutions of
   1998 and later, the 2008 GAFCON movement, and the Church of England's 2023 decision on blessings, to check), the United
   Methodist Church's separation and the Global Methodist Church (2022, to check), and the Presbyterian and Lutheran debates.
   Each denomination's own position is stated in its own words.
4. **Abuse and accountability (1990s to 2026):** the Catholic Church's crisis from the Boston revelations (2002) and the
   John Jay report (2004, to check); the Southern Baptist Convention's reports (2022, to check); and reforms and their
   limits. The page gives the findings and the responses, and says where the sources disagree.
5. **Renewal, charismatic and new movements (1989 to 2026):** the Pentecostal and charismatic growth in the global South,
   the Alpha course, and the spread of megachurches and independent churches, with their own critics.
6. **Ecumenism and its limits (1989 to 2026):** the Catholic–Orthodox dialogues, the Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of
   Justification (1999, Lutheran and Catholic), the World Council of Churches' work and its own divisions, and Pope
   Francis's and later popes' contacts with other churches (to check).
7. **Persecution and migration (1989 to 2026):** persecution in Nigeria, Pakistan, China, India and the Middle East;
   the emigration of Christians from Iraq and Syria; and migrant congregations in Europe and North America. Counts vary by
   source and the page must say so.
8. **Politics and the churches (1989 to 2026):** the evangelical vote in the United States, the "Christian nationalism"
   debate, the churches' role in post-communist politics, and the Catholic Church's social teaching on migration and the
   environment (*Laudato Si'*, 2015, to check).
9. **Where the church stands (the present):** a short closing section that sets out the main open questions. It gives
   no prediction, and says what would settle each question.

## Process and site work

Same as the earlier eras: choose editions and record licences first; check each citation as written with `scripts/cite.py`;
keep the ledger as we go (`docs/REVIEW-the-church-today.md`); set `reviewStatus` from the first commit, and record the
date each figure was checked. This era needs the most careful source work of all, because its figures change and many of its
sources are recent or in copyright. Because it is the last era, the `endYear` and the "as of" date should be checked at
each revision.

## Images (free images only)

Few images of the period are clearly free, and photographs of recent events are almost always in copyright. Plan one or two
images only where the licence is clear, such as a map or a public-domain photograph released before 1929 (not expected for
this era), or an image released under a free licence from Wikimedia Commons (for example, an open-licence photograph of a
modern church building). If none is clear, the page has no images and says so. Record each in `docs/IMAGES.md`.
