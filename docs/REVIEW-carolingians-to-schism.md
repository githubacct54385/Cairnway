The Catholic Encyclopedia and Gibbon are not primary sources. The encyclopedia's articles on Photius and Cerularius
are openly hostile to both; Gibbon is hostile to the image-venerating side. Both are used only for events and dates,
every use names them on the page, and their judgments are not repeated. No Eastern source (Photius's or Cerularius's
letters, the Russian Primary Chronicle) was read, and the page says so.

### What changed on the page

- **Corrected, misattribution:** the page said Einhard chapter 28 was "checked" and quoted him as saying the pope
  "declared Charlemagne emperor and defender of the Roman church, in the presence of the people and his knights", that
  Charlemagne "had no idea it was coming", received the title "without thanks", and feared the Greeks' envy. None of
  that is in Einhard. It is the Monk of St Gall, Book I, chapter 26, in the same volume, written about sixty years
  later and called "credulous" by Grant. The page now gives Einhard's own words and the Liber Pontificalis account of
  the crowning on Christmas Day.
- **Corrected, dates:** icons restored "843" became 842 (the Catholic Encyclopedia's date; the only one the sources
  read give). Photius "858 to 867" became "857 or 858 to 867" (the encyclopedia gives both). The Holy Sepulchre "1009"
  became "1009 or 1010" (Gibbon's note against the encyclopedia). The Libri Carolini "around 790 to 794" became "about
  790 to 792".
- **Corrected, 1054:** Leo IX died in April 1054, before his legates acted in July; the page now says so. "He answered
  by excommunicating the legates" was removed: no source read reports the patriarch's counter-excommunication.
  "Widely cited as the formal break" and "scholars debate" became Gibbon's own dating of "the consummation of the
  schism", with his note that friendly letters were later exchanged.
- **Corrected, Sutri:** the sources disagree on how the three claimants were removed; both versions are given.
- **Removed:**
  - Charlemagne's "common liturgy" (the encyclopedia credits Alcuin's Mass-book instead, and the page says so);
  - Otto I "drew the church into his government by appointing bishops", a practice "later reformers would attack";
  - "the middle kingdom ... was the source of many later disputes";
  - Cluny "became the center of a wide reforming movement" and "Its monks kept a demanding liturgy";
  - "Their mission is an early case of the church using a vernacular language";
  - "the dispute is often called the Photian schism";
  - "the account of how he chose Byzantine Christianity" (Vladimir);
  - "Many manuscripts from this period survive" and "the extent and results of the reforms are debated";
  - the ivory captions' "Ivories like this were made for courts and churches", "a crown and a scepter" (it is a
    staff topped with a cross), and "the travelers ... meeting the risen Christ".
- **Added from the sources:** the council of Frankfurt (794); the Byzantine recognition of 812; Grant's note on the
  meaning of the regret; Cyril and Methodius in 863; the Filioque's history from Toledo to Rome, with Leo III's advice
  to leave it out of the creed; Otto I and John XII in 963; the Annales Romani on Henry III's right to choose popes.
- **Map:** Bari and Venice, which the page does not mention, were removed. "Boundaries are simplified" became "No
  borders are drawn". A credit for Pleiades and Commons was added.
