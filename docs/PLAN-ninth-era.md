# Plan: The Ninth Era (AD 1417 to 1517)

> Drafted 2026-11-12 as the planning document for the ninth era, between the eighth era (AD 1215 to 1417) and the
> Reformation era (AD 1517 to 1648). Planning only.
> Every date, passage reference and book detail is from the drafter's general knowledge and is **U** (unverified) until
> checked, unless marked **F** (read in the full text of a named public-domain edition).

Proposed slug: `late-medieval-church` (file `src/content/eras/late-medieval-church.md`, `order: 9`). Title is a suggestion.

## Why this era

The eighth era ends with the election of Martin V in 1417, and the Reformation era begins with Luther in 1517. Without this
era, the site jumps a hundred years. The period is the church before the Reformation: a papacy that had survived the
councils and was rebuilding its power, a Europe of councils, Hussite wars and Renaissance courts, the fall of
Constantinople, and a growing demand for reform that Luther later answered. It is the era that explains why Luther's
complaints were heard.

## Decisions (owner, to confirm)

1. **Start: AD 1417** (election of Martin V). Consistent with the end of the eighth era.
2. **End: AD 1517** (Luther's Ninety-Five Theses, 31 October). Alternative: 1516 (Erasmus's Greek New Testament), but
   that would overlap the first part of the Reformation era.
3. **Length: 100 years.** This is the shortest era on the site. It is defensible because it begins and ends on clear
   events, but the owner could fold it into the eighth or the tenth era. This plan assumes it stays separate.
4. **East is in scope in a limited way**: the fall of Constantinople (1453), the Council of Florence and its failed union
   (1439), and the Ottoman advance into the Balkans. Keep the Eastern material to a few rows.

## Structure of the page

1. **The councils and their failure (1417 to 1449):** Martin V's reforms, the Council of Basel, and the struggle between
   pope and council (conciliarism).
2. **Hussites and the Bohemian settlement (1415 to 1436):** the war after Hus's death, and the Compacts of Basel (1433).
3. **The union of East and West (1438 to 1453):** the Council of Florence and the fall of Constantinople.
4. **The Renaissance papacy (1447 to 1517):** Nicholas V, Sixtus IV, Alexander VI, Julius II and the rebuilding of St
   Peter's.
5. **Piety and reform from below (1400 to 1500):** the Devotio Moderna, printing, and the preachers who called for reform
   (including Savonarola).
6. **The road to 1517:** the indulgence campaign for St Peter's and the questions it raised.

## Candidate people (about 10)

Martin V, Eugenius IV, Nicholas V, Pius II (Aeneas Silvius Piccolomini, who wrote about the councils), Jan Hus's
successors (Jan Rokycana), Mehmed II, Thomas à Kempis (*The Imitation of Christ*), Sixtus IV, Alexander VI, Julius II,
Girolamo Savonarola. Possible: Nicholas of Cusa (conciliar theory and the "learned ignorance"), Erasmus (as an
introduction to the next era).
