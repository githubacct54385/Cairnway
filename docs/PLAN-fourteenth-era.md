Proposed slug: `council-and-cold-war` (file `src/content/eras/council-and-cold-war.md`, `order: 14`). Title is a suggestion.

## Why this boundary

The thirteenth era ends with the war. This era begins there and ends in 1989, the year the Berlin Wall fell and the communist
governments of central Europe gave way. It covers the Cold War churches, the Second Vatican Council (1962 to 1965), and the
new ecumenical and global church that followed. The 1989 end is chosen because the history after it is still close to us,
and its sources and people are not yet settled. That history is best left for a later era.

## Decisions (owner, to confirm)

1. **Start: AD 1945.**
2. **End: AD 1989** (the fall of the Berlin Wall). **Decided:** 1989, leaving 1989 onwards for a later era, so the page does
   not have to give a verdict on living
   figures.
3. **Length: 44 years.** Shorter than most eras, so it can have more detail on each event.
4. **East is in scope**, with the Eastern churches under communism: the Russian Orthodox Church under Soviet control, the
   Romanian and Bulgarian churches, and the Ecumenical Patriarch's dialogue with Rome.
5. **Organised by theme**: the churches under communism, the Council, its reception, the global church, and the end of the
   Cold War.

## Structure of the page

1. **Reconstruction and the new ecumenism (1945 to 1958):** the World Council of Churches (Amsterdam, 1948), the Lutheran
   World Federation, Pius XII and the definition of the Assumption (1950).
2. **The churches under communism (1945 to 1989):** the arrests of Mindszenty in Hungary and Wyszyński in Poland, the
   Russian Orthodox Church's relationship with the state, the Romanian and Bulgarian churches, and the underground churches.
3. **The Second Vatican Council (1962 to 1965):** John XXIII's opening, the four constitutions (*Dei Verbum*, *Lumen Gentium*,
   *Sacrosanctum Concilium*, *Gaudium et Spes*), *Nostra Aetate* on other religions, and the lifting of the 1054 excommunications.
4. **The reception of the Council (1965 to 1978):** the liturgy in the vernacular, *Humanae Vitae* (1968), the dialogue
   between the churches, and the papacies of Paul VI and John Paul I.
5. **A global church (1968 to 1989):** the Medellín conference (1968), liberation theology, Oscar Romero's murder (1980), and
   the growth of Pentecostal and African churches.
6. **Pope John Paul II and the end of the Cold War (1978 to 1989):** the Polish pilgrimage (1979), Solidarity (1980), and the
   revolutions of 1989.
