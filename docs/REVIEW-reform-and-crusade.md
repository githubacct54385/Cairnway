Schaff's volume is a Protestant church history and the Catholic Encyclopedia a Catholic reference work; each has
open sympathies (Schaff on Canossa and Innocent III, the encyclopedia on Becket and the papacy). Both are used for
events and dates, and where a judgment is given it is attributed by name and set beside another. No Muslim or Byzantine
source was read, and the page says the sources are Western.

### What changed on the page

- **Corrected, dates:** Abelard's condemnation at Sens "1140" became 1141, as both sources say. The page now gives the
  dates on which the sources disagree side by side: Hattin, 4 or 5 July 1187; Jerusalem, 17 September or 2 October
  1187; Nicaea, 1 or 19 June 1097; Francis approved in 1209 or 1210; Lateran IV, 800 or 900 abbots and priors.
- **Corrected, Lateran IV:** the source is now Schaff's Latin of canon 1 ("transubstantiatis pane in corpus") and
  Thatcher and McNeal's note on canon 21, not Schroeder's translation on the Fordham site, whose copyright status was
  never recorded and which was not read again.
- **Corrected, people:** Becket's murderers "had heard the king's anger" became "four knights came from France", with
  the encyclopedia's note that Henry's responsibility is not clear. Innocent III's role "is debated" became what the
  sources record: he excommunicated the crusaders over Zara, threatened them over Constantinople, and condemned the
  sack.
- **Removed:**
  - the 1054 event, which belongs to the sixth era, and "scholars debate how lasting the break was";
  - Worms "ending the Investiture Controversy" (Schaff: a compromise);
  - Canossa "is often told as a victory", now Schaff's and Ogg's judgments by name;
  - Jerusalem "the size and treatment of the population are debated", now the encyclopedia's statement and Schaff's
    note on the chroniclers' exaggeration;
  - "the Latin empire ... and the restoration of 1261 are not yet checked", now Schaff's 1204 to 1261;
  - the friars' "significance for the church's pastoral work is debated";
  - "Caskets like this held relics, and were made in the workshops of Limoges", now from the museum record.
- **Added from the sources:** Worms 1076; Piacenza 1095; Bernard and the Jews of the Rhineland; Clarendon 1164;
  Henry II's penance; Innocent III's letter on the sack; the four witnesses of Urban's speech.

### What changed on the maps

- **Fourth Crusade, route corrected:** the map routed the fleet by Modon. Villehardouin names Modon only as the place
  where the Flemish fleet was told to meet the host and never came. The fleet went from Corfu by Malea to Negropont,
  Andros and Abydos. The route is now Venice, Zara, Corfu, Negropont, Constantinople. "Negroponte, taken by Venice after
  the sack" became the fleet's stop there in 1203, which Villehardouin records. The landing at Scutari was on 24 June
  1203, not "in July".
- **Third Crusade:** Arsuf (7 September 1191) was removed, because no source read mentions it. Acre's surrender is
  dated 13 July 1191. Limassol is now labelled Cyprus, since the sources name the island, not the port.
- **Second Crusade:** the route by Dorylaeum and Laodicea, the German defeat at Dorylaeum in October 1147, and the
  French losses on the road to Attalia were removed: no source read gives them. The route is now Schaff's: the kings
  met at Nicaea and went on to Ephesus, and Louis sailed from Attalia to Antioch.
- **Lisbon:** the fleet's route by Dartmouth and Porto, and the "July" start of the siege, were removed: the
  *De expugnatione Lyxbonensi* was not read. The map now marks only Lisbon, with the encyclopedia's account.
- **First Crusade:** the dates are now the encyclopedia's, which agree with the old notes except Nicaea, where the
  sources differ.
- **Overview map:** Cluny and Bari, which the page does not mention, were replaced by Cîteaux; "Boundaries are
  simplified" became "No borders are drawn".
- **Positions corrected:** Heraclea was 96 km off, near Karaman, not at Ereğli (Heraclea Cybistra); Ascalon was 9 km
  inland of the ancient site; Hattin was 9 km from the Horns of Hattin; Canossa was 4 km from the castle.
- **Refs:** every map note named a chronicle "to check" (Gesta Francorum, Raymond of Aguilers, Fulcher, Ambroise, Odo
  of Deuil, William of Tyre). None of them was read in full for the maps, so each note now names the source actually
  read.
