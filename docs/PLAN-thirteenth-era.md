# Plan: The Thirteenth Era (AD 1870 to 1945)

> Drafted 2026-11-12 as the planning document for the thirteenth era, after the twelfth era (AD 1789 to 1870). Planning
> only. Every date, passage reference and book detail is from the drafter's general knowledge and is **U** (unverified)
> until checked, unless marked **F** (read in the full text of a named public-domain edition).

Proposed slug: `church-in-the-modern-world` (file `src/content/eras/church-in-the-modern-world.md`, `order: 13`). Title is a
suggestion.

## Why this boundary

The twelfth era ends with Vatican I and the loss of Rome. This era begins there. It ends in 1945, the end of the Second
World War in Europe, which left the churches of Europe and Asia in new political settings: the Iron Curtain, the Holocaust's
aftermath, and the early ecumenical movement's new institutions. The 1945 end is a natural break, but the Holocaust and the
churches' responses to it are central, so the page must treat them with care (see Decisions).

## Decisions (owner, to confirm)

1. **Start: AD 1870.**
2. **End: AD 1945** (the end of the war in Europe). The alternative is 1939 (the start of the war), which would cut the page
   short. Not recommended.
3. **Length: 75 years.** The longest era so far. The page should be organised by theme, with the ecumenical and Eastern
   material given their own sections so they do not get lost.
4. **East is in scope in full**: the Russian church and the revolution, the Armenian genocide, the Greek and Turkish exchange
   of populations, and the Ecumenical Patriarchate. This is a larger share than in the twelfth era.
5. **The Holocaust and the churches (decided):** the page will say what the sources show about church responses in Germany
   and elsewhere, including the criticism of Pius XII and the defences made for him. It will set out the debate and give no
   verdict the sources do not support. The frontier-model review will need to check this section.

## Structure of the page

1. **Leo XIII and the social question (1878 to 1903):** *Aeterni Patris* (1879), *Rerum Novarum* (1891), and the Roman
   question after 1870.
2. **Modernism and its condemnation (1890 to 1914):** Harnack's lectures on the essence of Christianity, Loisy and Tyrrell,
   and *Pascendi* (1907).
3. **Missions and empire (1870 to 1914):** the Catholic and Protestant missions in Africa and Asia, the Edinburgh Missionary
   Conference (1910), and the Boxer martyrs (1900).
4. **The First World War (1914 to 1918):** the chaplains, the Christmas truce (1914, to check), Benedict XV's peace appeal
   (1917), and the Armenian genocide (from 1915).
5. **Revolution in the East (1917 to 1924):** the Russian church's council and Patriarch Tikhon (1917), the Soviet decree
   separating church and state (1918), persecution in the 1920s, and the Lausanne population exchange (1923).
6. **Fascism, totalitarianism and the churches (1922 to 1945):** the Lateran Treaties (1929), the Reich Concordat (1933), the
   Barmen Declaration (1934), the Spanish Civil War, and the churches' responses to the Holocaust.
7. **Scripture and the new ecumenism (1943 to 1945):** *Divino Afflante Spiritu* (1943), the ecumenical plans for a world
   council, and the deaths of Dietrich Bonhoeffer and others.

## Modern books (to choose; owner needs library copies)

- Richard Evans, *The Third Reich in Power* (2005). Germany and the churches in the 1930s.
- Ian Kershaw, *Hitler* (two volumes, 1998 and 2000). Background on the regime.
- Hugh McLeod, *Religion and the People of Western Europe, 1789 to 1989* (1997). The framework for the whole period.
- A study of the Russian church's history in the Soviet period, and one on the Armenian genocide (candidates to check).

Recommendation: McLeod as the framework, with Evans and Kershaw for Germany. All titles and years are from memory and must be
verified.

## Process and site work

Same as the earlier eras: choose editions and record licences first; check each citation as written with `scripts/cite.py`;
keep the ledger as we go (`docs/REVIEW-church-in-the-modern-world.md`); set `reviewStatus` from the first commit. This era
needs the most careful source work, because of the Holocaust and the Armenian genocide sections and the copyright of many
20th-century texts.

## Images (period art and photographs, public domain only)

Most photographs of 1914 to 1945 are still in copyright outside the United States, and many are not public domain even in the
United States. Plan two or three images that are clearly free: a pre-1929 print or painting of the Vatican or the missions, a
Russian Orthodox icon from the period (check the Met or another museum), and a photograph of the Lateran Treaties or Barmen
only if its licence is clear. Record each in `docs/IMAGES.md`. Where no image is clearly free, use none and say so on the
page.
