## Overview

The twelfth era ended with Vatican I and the fall of Rome. This era runs from that point to the end of the Second World War in
Europe in 1945. In those seventy-five years, the papacy set out a new position against modern ideas and then began to answer
the social question. Missions spread across Africa and Asia. The First World War and the Armenian genocide changed the East,
and the Russian Revolution began a long persecution of the churches there. In Germany, Italy and Spain, the churches faced
regimes that wanted their loyalty. The page is arranged by theme, and the Eastern churches and the 1930s are given their own
sections.

## Leo XIII and the social question

Leo XIII was elected in 1878. *Aeterni Patris* (1879) called for the study of St Thomas Aquinas, and *Rerum Novarum* (1891)
addressed labour, property and the rights of workers. The encyclical is usually seen as the start of modern Catholic social
teaching. The Roman question, the dispute over the pope's position after 1870, remained open until 1929. Details not yet
checked.

## Modernism and its condemnation

Adolf von Harnack's lectures in Berlin, published as *What Is Christianity?*, asked what was essential in the faith and what
was historical accident. Alfred Loisy and George Tyrrell applied historical criticism to Catholic belief. *Pascendi Dominici
gregis* (1907) condemned modernism as a whole system, and the church set up procedures against suspected modernists. Details
not yet checked.

## Missions and empire

Catholic and Protestant missionaries spread widely in Africa, Asia and the Pacific in this period, often alongside colonial
rule. The Edinburgh Missionary Conference of 1910 is usually seen as the start of the modern ecumenical movement. In 1900 the
Boxer rising in China killed many missionaries and Chinese Christians. Details not yet checked.

## The First World War

Chaplains served on both sides of the war. On the Western Front, a truce on Christmas 1914 is reported in some places; its
extent is debated. Benedict XV called for peace in August 1917, a call that was not heeded. Details not yet checked.

## The Armenian genocide

The Ottoman government's campaign against its Armenian population began in April 1915, with the arrest of Armenian leaders in
Constantinople, and led to the deaths of a very large number of Armenians. The church's role in the events and its response are
part of the history and need a specialist. Details not yet checked.

## Revolution in the East

The Russian Revolution of 1917 brought the Russian Orthodox Church a council, which elected Patriarch Tikhon in November 1917. In
January 1918 a Soviet decree separated church and state, and the church was then persecuted through the 1920s and 1930s. The
Treaty of Lausanne (1923) led to a compulsory exchange of Greek and Turkish populations, many of them Orthodox or Muslim by
faith. Details not yet checked.

## Fascism, totalitarianism and the churches

The Lateran Treaties of 1929 settled the Roman question by creating Vatican City as a state. The Reich Concordat of 1933 set the
terms between the Holy See and Germany. The Barmen Declaration of 1934 was the founding statement of the Confessing Church, which
opposed the Nazi takeover of the Protestant churches. In Spain the civil war of 1936 to 1939 saw many clergy and religious killed.
Details not yet checked.

## Pius XII and the Holocaust

Pius XII was elected in March 1939. Historians disagree about what the papacy and the German and other churches knew of the
Holocaust, and about what they could have done. Critics argue that the pope's public silence and diplomacy failed the victims.
Defenders argue that his private efforts saved lives and that open protest would have made things worse. The further reading below
covers the period in general; a dedicated study of the debate is still to be chosen. This page sets out the debate and gives no verdict the sources do not
support. Details not yet checked.

## Scripture and the new ecumenism

*Divino Afflante Spiritu* (1943) allowed Catholic scholars wider use of historical and textual methods in studying Scripture. The
ecumenical movement that began at Edinburgh was preparing the World Council of Churches, which was founded in 1948. Dietrich
Bonhoeffer, a Lutheran pastor and member of the German resistance, was hanged at Flossenbürg on 9 April 1945. Details not yet
checked.

## Key debates

### Did modernism deserve condemnation?

Supporters of the 1907 condemnation saw it as a defence of the faith. Critics, including some Catholic scholars, saw it as a
suppression of legitimate study. The page does not yet give a view.

### What did the churches know, and what did they do, about the Holocaust?

This is the most contested question on the page. See the section above. The page does not yet give a view, and the debate is
set out in the sources.

### Was the Armenian genocide a matter for the churches?

The churches of the Ottoman Empire were involved in different ways, and the Western churches made statements in its aftermath.
How much they did is debated. The page does not yet give a view.
