## Overview

The twelfth era ended with the First Vatican Council and the fall of Rome in 1870. This era runs from that point to the end of the Second World War in Europe in 1945. In those seventy-five years Leo XIII called Catholics back to St Thomas Aquinas and set out a Catholic answer to the social question, and Pius X condemned modernism. Missionaries were killed in China's Boxer rebellion. The First World War brought the massacre of the Armenians of Asia Minor, and the Russian Revolution brought persecution of the Russian church. The popes made treaties with Mussolini's Italy and Hitler's Germany, and in 1937 Pius XI accused Germany of breaking its treaty.

The page is arranged by theme. Events are tagged East or West. The Protestant churches and the churches of the 1930s are covered in less detail than the draft planned, because few sources on them could be read for this check.

## Leo XIII and the social question

The Catholic Encyclopedia (1913) records that Leo XIII was elected on 20 February 1878. His encyclical *Aeterni Patris* of 4 August 1879 called for the revival of Scholastic philosophy "according to the mind of St. Thomas Aquinas", whom it named the prince of the Scholastic theologians and philosophers. *Rerum Novarum* of 18 May 1891, in the Encyclopedia's words, "set forth with profound erudition the Christian principles bearing on the relations between capital and labour, and it gave a vigorous impulse to the social movement along Christian lines". The Roman question, the dispute over the pope's position after 1870, stayed open until 1929.

## Modernism and its condemnation

In the winter term of 1899 to 1900 Adolf Harnack lectured at Berlin to some six hundred students on the essence of Christianity. His translator records that a listener took the lectures down in shorthand, and the report became the book, published in English as *What Is Christianity?* in 1901. Harnack sought what was "essential" in the faith, to "distinguish kernel and husk".

Among Catholics, the Catholic Encyclopedia names Alfred Loisy and George Tyrrell among the modernists. In 1907 Pius X condemned sixty-five propositions in the decree *Lamentabili*, most of them on Scripture. On 8 September 1907 the encyclical *Pascendi* condemned modernism as a system and set out rules to combat it. It asked whether anyone could be surprised "that We should define it as the synthesis of all heresies". Pius X later required those who taught or held office in the church to take an oath rejecting the errors condemned in the two documents.

## Missions and empire

In 1900 the Boxer rebellion began in Shandong and spread to Zhili. The Catholic Encyclopedia says that "everywhere missionaries were murdered", and that the legations at Peking were besieged. In 1910 a world missionary conference met at Edinburgh.

## The First World War

Benedict XV was elected on 3 September 1914, a few weeks after the war began. The 1926 Britannica records that he "preserved an attitude of strict neutrality, and made several attempts to effect peace". During his reign official relations between France and the Vatican were resumed, and a British representative was accredited to the papal court for the first time since the 17th century.

## The Armenian genocide

The 1922 Britannica describes the Ottoman policy toward the Armenians of Asia Minor as "deportation coupled with extermination". Those able to march were to be deported to Mesopotamia and eastern Syria. Armenians in provinces too far away for deportation were to be killed or driven into hiding. It says that the "exterminatory measures" were "redoubled at the beginning of April 1915", and that on 24 May 1915 the British and French governments declared that they would hold Ottoman ministers personally responsible.

## Revolution in the East

The 1922 Britannica records that after the Revolution of 1917 a national council of the Russian Orthodox Church restored the patriarchate of Moscow "after an interval of 200 years". The new patriarch, Tikhon, "denounced and anathematized the Communists". The Bolsheviks "turned sanctuaries into public halls, desecrated revered shrines, tortured priests and shot bishops". In Britannica's judgment, these persecutions strengthened the church's hold on its people.

A convention signed at Lausanne on 30 January 1923 provided for the exchange of Greek and Turkish populations.

## Fascism, totalitarianism and the churches

In the Lateran treaty of 11 February 1929 the Holy See declared the Roman question definitively and irrevocably settled. It recognized the Kingdom of Italy, with Rome as its capital, and Italy recognized the State of Vatican City under the sovereignty of the pope.

In 1933 Pius XI agreed to a concordat with Germany. In *Mit brennender Sorge*, dated 14 March 1937, he wrote that the Reich government had proposed it, and that he had agreed "despite many and grave misgivings" to spare German Catholics worse trials. He then accused Germany of treating "its more or less official violation as a normal policy". The encyclical condemned anyone who "exalts race, or the people, or the State" and "divinizes them to an idolatrous level".

## Pius XII and the Holocaust

Pius XII was elected on 2 March 1939. What he and the churches knew of the Holocaust, and whether they did enough against it, is among the most contested questions in the history of the modern church. No source on that debate could be read for this check. This page therefore names the question but does not summarize the arguments.

## Scripture and the Protestant churches

*Divino Afflante Spiritu*, dated 30 September 1943, urged Catholic interpreters of Scripture to master the biblical languages, and defended the use of the original texts "in accordance with the methods of criticism". It also asked them to work out the "forms or kinds of speech" used by the ancient writers of the East. Wikidata records that Dietrich Bonhoeffer, a Lutheran pastor and theologian of the Confessing Church and a member of the German resistance, was hanged at the Flossenbürg concentration camp on 9 April 1945.

## Key debates

### Did modernism deserve condemnation?

*Pascendi* called modernism "the synthesis of all heresies". The Catholic Encyclopedia's article on modernism defends the condemnation. The same article quotes Loisy's own account of the modernists as "a fairly definite group of thinking men united in the common desire to adapt Catholicism to the intellectual, moral and social needs of today".

### Was the concordat with Germany a mistake?

Pius XI's own account, in *Mit brennender Sorge*, is that he signed it to protect the church's freedom in Germany, despite "many and grave misgivings", and that Germany then broke it. No other view could be read for this check.

### What did the churches know, and what did they do, about the Holocaust?

See the section on Pius XII above. The page gives no verdict.
