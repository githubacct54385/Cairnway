## Overview

The eighth era ended when the Council of Constance restored one pope in 1417. This era runs from that point to Luther's Ninety-Five Theses in 1517. In those hundred years, the councils tried to reform the papacy and failed to control it, the Hussite wars divided Bohemia, the Eastern and Western churches tried to reunite and then lost Constantinople, and the popes of the Renaissance rebuilt Rome as a centre of art and power. Many people wanted reform, and some of them, such as Savonarola, were punished for it.

## Key debates

### Should councils rule the church?

The conciliar movement held that a general council could reform the church and even depose a pope. Pope Eugenius IV and his successors resisted this. Historians debate how far the councils were a real alternative to the papacy. The page does not yet give a view.

### Did the union of Florence matter?

The decree of union was agreed in 1439, but Constantinople did not accept it in practice, and the city fell in 1453. Whether the union was ever a real settlement is debated. The page does not yet settle this.

### Was the Renaissance papacy a cause of reform?

The worldliness of the papal court was often attacked, and the indulgence campaign of 1517 is usually seen as the spark for Luther. Whether the Renaissance papacy caused the reform movement, or only gave it a target, is debated. The page does not yet give a view.

## Worship and community life

### Devotion and the Devotio Moderna

Groups such as the Brethren of the Common Life encouraged a simple, inward piety, and taught in schools. The Imitation of Christ was widely read. Details not yet checked.

### Printing

Printing from movable type spread quickly in the 1450s and after. It changed how books, prayers and sermons reached readers. Details not yet checked.

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning on this page |

| Conciliarism | The view that a general council stands above the pope |
| Indulgence | A remission of the temporal punishment for sin, granted by the church |
