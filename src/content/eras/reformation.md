## Overview

The ninth era ended with Luther's Ninety-Five Theses. This era runs from those theses in 1517 to the Peace of Westphalia in 1648. In those years, a dispute about indulgences became a movement that split western Christendom. Princes and cities chose their churches, England broke from Rome, the Catholic Church reformed itself at the Council of Trent, and the wars of religion tore Europe apart. The peace of 1648 settled the German lands for a while, but it did not end the division of the churches.

## Key debates

### Why did the Reformation succeed where earlier reforms failed?

Earlier calls for reform, such as those of Hus and Wycliffe, did not split the church. Historians debate whether printing, the princes, or the content of Luther's teaching made the difference. The page does not yet give a view.

### Could the church have been reformed without a split?

Erasmus and others wanted reform from within. The Council of Trent tried to reform the Catholic Church, but it did not reunite the churches. Whether a settlement was ever possible is debated. The page does not yet settle this.

### Did religion cause the wars?

The wars of religion in France and the Netherlands, and the Thirty Years' War, all had religious and political causes. Historians disagree on how far religion was the main cause. The page does not yet take a position.

## Worship and community life

### Preaching and the Bible in the vernacular

Luther's translation of the Bible into German, and the use of the vernacular in worship, changed what ordinary people could read and hear. Details not yet checked.

### Monasteries and the orders

Many monasteries were dissolved in Protestant lands and in England. The Jesuits and other new orders took up preaching and teaching in Catholic lands. Details not yet checked.
