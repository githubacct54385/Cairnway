## Overview

The ninth era ended with Luther's Ninety-Five Theses. This era runs from those theses in 1517 to the Peace of Westphalia in 1648. In those years a dispute about indulgences became a movement that split western Christendom. Princes and cities chose their churches, England broke from Rome, the Catholic Church held the Council of Trent, and wars of religion ran through France, the empire and beyond. The peace of 1648 settled the German lands, but it did not end the division of the churches.

## Key debates

### Why did the Reformation succeed where earlier reforms failed?

The sources read here point to several things without weighing them. Schaff gives a section to the printing press, and Luther's German New Testament appeared in 1522. Princes protected the reform: Schaff describes how each ruler who adopted it took charge of the church in his lands. And Luther's teaching on justification by faith is at the center of his account. The page does not choose among them.

### Could the church have been reformed without a split?

At Marburg the Lutheran and Swiss reformers agreed on fourteen articles of fifteen but could not agree on the Lord's Supper. At Augsburg in 1530 the emperor heard the Lutheran confession, and the Catholic theologians answered it with a confutation. The Council of Trent reformed the Catholic Church but did not reunite the churches. The sources read here do not say whether a settlement was ever possible.

### Did religion cause the wars?

The sources read here describe religious and political causes together. Schaff calls a massacre in the Valtellina in 1620 the work of "religious fanaticism combined with political discontent", and the Peace of Augsburg tied religion to territory. The page does not decide how far religion was the main cause.

## Worship and community life

### Preaching and the Bible in the vernacular

Luther's German New Testament appeared in September 1522 and his whole Bible in 1534, printed with many woodcuts; Schaff says one Wittenberg printer, Hans Lufft, sold about a hundred thousand copies between 1534 and 1574. At Zurich, Luther's New Testament was adapted to the Swiss dialect in 1524, and the whole Bible was published in German there by Froschauer in 1530, translated in part by Leo Jud and other Zurich divines.

### Monasteries and the orders

Henry VIII's commissioners visited the English monasteries, and Parliament began their suppression in 1536; the Pilgrimage of Grace rose against it that autumn. The Jesuits, approved in 1540, were among the new orders in Catholic lands.
