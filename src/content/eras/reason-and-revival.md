## Overview

The Reformation era ended with the Peace of Westphalia in 1648. This era runs from that peace to the French Revolution in 1789. In those years the churches of Europe lived with the division that the Reformation had made. Catholics renewed their faith in new ways, including the Jesuit missions and Jansenist debates over grace. Protestants turned inward to pietism and to revivals in Britain and America. Philosophers asked whether reason could support revelation at all. Rulers, from Peter the Great to Joseph II, tried to bring the church under state control, and the Revolution in France seized its lands.

## Key debates

### How much can reason prove about God?

Enlightenment thinkers such as Hume and Kant tested religious belief against reason. Historians debate how far the Enlightenment was an attack on religion and how far it was a religious reform. The page does not yet give a view.

### Did the Jesuit suppression come from the Enlightenment?

The Jesuits were expelled from several countries before 1773, under pressure from Catholic courts. Whether the suppression was mainly political, or was a sign of a wider change in attitudes, is debated. The page does not yet settle this.

### Did the state take over the church?

Peter the Great, Joseph II and the French revolutionaries all used the state's power over the church. Historians disagree on whether this was a break with the past or a continuation of the royal control already in place. The page does not yet give a view.

## Worship and community life

### Pietist groups and small meetings

Spener's collegia pietatis, and the Moravian and Methodist groups, met in homes to read the Bible and pray together. Their effect on parish life is debated. Details not yet checked.

### Parish life and devotion

Catholic devotion took many forms, from the Virgin of Good Counsel to processions and pilgrimages, and many of these were encouraged by bishops and religious orders. Details not yet checked.

| Jansenism | A movement in the French church that stressed grace and strict moral standards |
