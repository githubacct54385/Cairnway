## Overview

The Reformation era ended with the Peace of Westphalia in 1648. This era runs from that peace to the French Revolution in 1789. In those years the churches of Europe lived with the division the Reformation had made. In the Catholic Church the Jansenists and the Jesuits quarrelled over grace and morals, and the Catholic courts drove out the Jesuits until the pope suppressed them. Lutheran Pietists, the Moravians and the Methodists sought a more personal faith, and revival came to New England. Philosophers from Hume to Kant asked what reason could know of God. Rulers, from Louis XIV and Peter the Great to Joseph II, used their power over the churches, and in 1789 the French Assembly took the church's property.

## Key debates

### Pascal, the Jansenists and the Jesuits

The two main sources read here take opposite sides. Schaff calls Jansenism an earnest attempt at a conservative reform by reviving Augustine's teaching on sin and grace, and the destruction of Port-Royal "barbarous tyranny". The Catholic Encyclopedia says Pascal did not seriously alter his quotations from the casuists but arranged them somewhat disingenuously, and that he unjustly blamed the Society of Jesus alone; it also calls the Provincial Letters the first prose masterpiece of the French language. The page gives both views.

### Why were the Jesuits suppressed?

The Catholic Encyclopedia puts the suppression down to the Bourbon courts of France, Spain, Naples and Parma and to Pombal in Portugal, moved, it says, by the spirit of the times: "French irreligious philosophism", Jansenism, Gallicanism and Erastianism. It says the Spanish ambassador Moniño was responsible for the substance of the brief, and the pope gave it little more than its form. The page does not have a source with another view, and so gives this one with its name.

### Reason and revelation

Kant's critique of the proofs of God, the Catholic Encyclopedia says, did not deny God's existence. Schaff writes that the Methodist revival checked the "skepticism and infidelity which had begun to set in with deism", and that the destruction of Port-Royal "bred a generation of skeptics and infidels" in France. Hume's own arguments are not given here, because only the title page of his Dialogues was read.

### Did the state take over the church?

The Catholic Encyclopedia calls Peter's Synod a copy of the consistories of the German Protestant states, set up "to bring the Church into absolute dependence on the State". In France it says the Assembly of 1789 wanted the Catholic Church to be "subject to the State and really organized by the State". Joseph II, by contrast, appears in Schaff for the toleration he granted. The sources read here do not say whether this was something new or a continuation of earlier royal control.

## Worship and community life

### Pietist and Methodist meetings

Spener's collegia pietatis met in his house at Frankfurt from 1670 for devout reading and mutual edification. Schaff names the Methodists' own institutions: itinerant preaching, class-meetings, band-meetings, love-feasts and conferences. The Moravians of Herrnhut, he says, gave the first Methodists their cheerful trust in God.

### Devotion to Mary

Devotion to the image of Our Lady of Good Counsel at Genazzano spread in this period: the Catholic Encyclopedia says Innocent XI had the picture crowned in 1682, and Benedict XIII granted an office and Mass for its feast in 1727. The German devotional picture above names that image.
