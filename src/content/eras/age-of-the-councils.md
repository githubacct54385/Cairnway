## Overview

The previous era ended with toleration. This one starts there. In AD 313 the emperors Constantine and Licinius agreed that Christians could worship freely, and by 325 the bishops were meeting at Nicaea at the emperor's invitation (Eusebius, Life of Constantine 3.6). The questions the church argued over from then on were no longer how to survive but how to speak: who Christ is, which books to read in church, and what grace means.

The page is arranged by East and West, because the Greek-speaking and Latin-speaking halves of the empire argued about different things in different languages. In the East the main questions were about the Son and the Spirit and, later, about the person of Christ. In the West the main figures, Ambrose, Jerome and Augustine, wrote about church and state, Scripture, sin and grace.

This East and West is a matter of language and region. It is not the later split between the Roman Catholic and Orthodox churches. The splits that began in this era are different: Henry Chadwick describes how the Church of the East did not accept the council at Ephesus, and how the churches now called Oriental Orthodox did not accept Chalcedon.

## Key beliefs and debates

### What did the council at Nicaea decide?

It issued a creed that says the Son is begotten, not made, and of one substance with the Father, and it condemns those who say there was a time when he was not (The Seven Ecumenical Councils, the Nicene Creed). A letter in the emperor's name to those who were absent urges them to keep Easter on one day. The councils collection does not contain a list of Bible books from Nicaea; on this page the lists come from Athanasius in 367 and from the African church, below.

### Which books are in the Bible?

Lists differed. Athanasius's list for the New Testament matches the one most Christians use today: four Gospels, Acts, seven catholic letters, fourteen letters of Paul including Hebrews, and Revelation. He says other books, including the Shepherd and the Teaching of the Apostles, are not in the canon but may be read to new believers. The African canon's list also has the same New Testament books, but its Old Testament includes Tobit, Judith, the Maccabees and Ezra, and it says nothing but canonical Scripture is to be read in church. Churches still differ on the Old Testament books to this day. How the lists came about is debated; Harry Gamble's *The New Testament Canon* describes the different views.

### Who was Arius, and what did he teach?

On this page the Arian side is known only through the people who opposed it. The Nicene creed's condemnations, such as there was a time when he was not, show what the council took Arius's position to be. Henry Chadwick discusses how far the opponents' reports can be trusted, and Rowan Williams's *Arius* (Further reading) is a full study.

### One person in two natures

The council at Chalcedon says that the one Lord Jesus Christ is perfect in godhead and perfect in manhood, consubstantial with the Father and with us, and must be confessed in two natures, unconfusedly, immutably, indivisibly, inseparably. Leo's Tome, read at the council, says each form does what is proper to it with the co-operation of the other. Henry Chadwick describes how the definition was rejected by some churches, the ones now called Oriental Orthodox.

### Grace and free will

Augustine's prayer, give what you command and command what you will, came to stand for his side in the dispute with Pelagius. The editor of his anti-Pelagian writings says Pelagius held that people could do whatever God commanded, and was provoked when the prayer was repeated in his presence. Augustine says it appears in his Confessions, written before the dispute, and he argued that even the beginning of faith is God's gift (On the Predestination of the Saints 3). Christian traditions still differ on how to read the question.

### Did Constantine make the empire Christian?

The agreement of AD 313 gave Christians freedom, along with everyone else. The sources on this page show emperors calling councils, writing to bishops and, in Julian's case, turning back to the old religion; none of them is a law making Christianity the religion of the empire, so this page does not make that claim.

## Worship and community life

### Monks and the desert

Athanasius's Life says Antony was moved by hearing the Gospel passage in which Jesus tells a rich man to sell what he has and give to the poor. Augustine says the story was in his mind when he heard the voice in the garden. The Life shows a new kind of Christian life, lived apart in the desert.

### Bishops and emperors

Ambrose's letter to Theodosius shows a bishop telling an emperor that he dared not offer the sacrifice in his presence until he repented (Letter 51.13). The editors of Ambrose say the emperor did penance and was readmitted to communion.

### What was read in church

The African canon says that besides the canonical Scriptures nothing is to be read in church under the name of divine Scripture, and gives the list. The notes add that the earlier version at Hippo also allowed the accounts of the martyrs to be read on their anniversaries. Athanasius allows some books to be read to new believers that are not in the canon. The two together show that reading in church and the canon were not yet the same thing everywhere.

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning on this page |
| --- | --- |
| Apocrypha | Books outside the lists some churches accept as Scripture, such as Tobit and Judith in Athanasius's and the African lists |
