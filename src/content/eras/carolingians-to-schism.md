## Overview

The fifth era ended at the Second Council of Nicaea in 787. This era begins there, with the Frankish response to that council, and ends with the mutual excommunications of 1054. In between, a Frankish kingdom tried to become a Christian empire in the West, the empire broke up, the Eastern and Western churches argued over the papacy and the creed, and monks at Cluny led a reform that changed the Western church.

The years from 787 to 800 are the first part of this era. The Frankish court had not sent bishops to Nicaea, and it rejected the council's decree on images. The court's *Libri Carolini*, written around 790 to 794, set out that rejection. Charlemagne's coronation in Rome in 800 comes after these years. ## Key debates

### What did the coronation of 800 mean?

Einhard says Charlemagne was surprised by the title and would have preferred not to receive it so openly, because he feared the Greeks would react with hostility. Its meaning is debated: whether it was the pope's idea, whether it asserted papal authority over emperors, and how the Byzantine court received it. Einhard's account supports the Greek hostility only in part; this page does not yet settle these questions.

### Who should choose the pope?

The papacy was often controlled by local Roman families in the tenth century, and by German emperors in the eleventh. Reformers wanted the choice to rest with the clergy of Rome. The synod of 1046 and the reforms of Leo IX are part of that story, and the details are not yet checked.

### Should the Filioque be in the creed?

The Western churches added a phrase to the creed about the Holy Spirit proceeding from the Father and the Son. The East objected, and Photius made it a central complaint. The dispute is one of the main doctrinal reasons for the later schism. The page reports the dispute but does not yet give the Eastern and Western arguments in full.

## Worship and community life

### Monks and reform

Cluny's charter freed the monastery from local lords and placed it under the pope's protection. Its monks kept a demanding liturgy, and many houses adopted its customs. The movement reached far beyond Burgundy. Details not yet checked.

### Schools and books

The Carolingian reforms set out to correct the copying of texts and to improve Latin. Many manuscripts from this period survive. The extent and results of the reforms are debated.
