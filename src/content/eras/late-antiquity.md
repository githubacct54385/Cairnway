## Overview

The third era ended with the council at Chalcedon, which left the church divided in the East over the person of Christ. This era starts there. Over the next century and a half the western empire lost its emperors, its armies and most of its provinces. The eastern empire, with its capital at Constantinople, held together under Justinian, but it did not recover the West. In the West, Christianity outlived the empire that had once protected it. Bishops, monks and kings took up the work of government and learning, and the Bishop of Rome, Gregory the Great, became one of the most powerful men in Italy.

The page is arranged by East and West, as before. Each event says which region it belongs to. Where a story belongs to both, it is marked "East and West".

The churches of this era did not yet have the separations of later centuries. The split between Roman Catholic and Orthodox churches is conventionally dated to AD 1054. The splits that begin in this era are the Church of the East (after Ephesus, AD 431) and the Oriental Orthodox churches (after Chalcedon, AD 451). Those separate churches are not a mistake to correct later; they are part of the story.

## Key beliefs and debates

### Did Chalcedon heal the church?

No. The council at Chalcedon, in AD 451, defined the two natures of Christ. Churches in Egypt and Syria refused the definition and kept their own bishops. Justinian tried to bring them back. The council he called in 553 condemned three earlier writings, which turned into an argument about whether a dead bishop could be condemned for heresy. The argument mattered most to the bishops, who did not all agree.

### Was the western empire's end sudden?

Traditional histories date the end of the western empire to AD 476, when Odoacer deposed the last emperor. Modern historians tend to describe a gradual change, with Germanic kings and Roman officials working together. The page does not yet check the scholarly view in detail; see Further reading.

### What did the Pastoral Rule ask of a bishop?

Gregory's Pastoral Rule sets out the qualities a bishop needs, and it gives advice for leading people of different kinds. It says the ruler of souls must distinguish virtues from vices with careful attention, so that he is not misled by meanness that looks like thrift, or by an appearance of generosity. It was copied and read for centuries.

### Did the English become Christian through Gregory?

Gregory's letters show him sending Augustine of Canterbury and others to the English, and rejoicing in the conversion of the Angli. Bede, writing in the eighth century, tells the story in detail, but he wrote a century later. Historians debate how much the Christians already in Britain and the Irish mission contributed. The page does not yet decide this.

## Worship and community life

### Monks and the Rule

Benedict's Rule for monks set out prayer at fixed hours, manual work, reading, and obedience to an abbot. Its influence grew slowly in the West. The page does not yet quote the Rule itself.

### Bishops and the state

In the West, bishops such as Gregory ran the church's estates, fed the poor and wrote to kings. In the East, the emperor called councils and took a strong part in church affairs. The difference between these roles is an important part of the story, and it is not yet checked against the sources.

## Islam: a note, not part of this era

Islam began in Arabia after AD 600. Muhammad is said to have begun to preach in Mecca around AD 610, and within a few decades the Arab armies had taken Syria, Egypt and much of the Persian empire. Those lands had large Christian populations, and their churches were among those that had parted from the Chalcedon definition. This is outside the era, and the dates and details here have not been checked.
