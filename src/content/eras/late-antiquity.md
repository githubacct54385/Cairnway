## Overview

The third era ended with the council at Chalcedon, which left the church divided in the East over the person of Christ. This era starts there. Over the next century and a half the West lost its emperors: Procopius tells how the young emperor Augustulus was set aside in Italy. The eastern empire, with its capital at Constantinople, held together, and Justinian says his armies brought Africa and other provinces back under Rome. In the West, bishops, monks and kings took up the work of government and learning, and the letters of Gregory the Great show a bishop of Rome managing estates and negotiating with a king.

The page is arranged by East and West, as before. Each event says which region it belongs to. Where a story belongs to both, it is marked "East and West".

The later split between the Roman Catholic and Orthodox churches had not happened. The splits on this page are older ones: the Church of the East, after the council at Ephesus (AD 431), and the churches now called Oriental Orthodox, after Chalcedon (AD 451), as Henry Chadwick describes. Those separate churches are not a mistake to correct later; they are part of the story.

## Key beliefs and debates

### Did Chalcedon heal the church?

No. The council at Chalcedon, in AD 451, defined the two natures of Christ. Churches in Egypt and Syria refused the definition and kept their own bishops. Henry Chadwick describes Justinian's attempts to bring them back. The council he called in 553 condemned three earlier writings. Its record shows the argument turning on whether the letter of Ibas had been approved at Chalcedon, and Pope Vigilius first resisted and then gave way.

### Was the western empire's end sudden?

The year usually given is AD 476, when, Procopius says, Odoacer set the young emperor aside and let him live as a private citizen. Procopius tells it as a change of ruler, not the end of a world: Odoacer gave a third of Italy to the barbarian allies and ruled for ten years. Henry Chadwick describes the end of the western empire as a gradual change rather than a sudden fall.

### What did the Pastoral Rule ask of a bishop?

Gregory's Pastoral Rule sets out the qualities a bishop needs, and it gives advice for leading people of different kinds. It says the ruler of souls must distinguish virtues from vices with careful attention, so that he is not misled by meanness that looks like thrift, or by an appearance of generosity. It was copied and read for centuries.

### Did the English become Christian through Gregory?

Gregory's letters show him sending Augustine of Canterbury and others to the English, and rejoicing in the conversion of the Angli. Bede, who finished his history in 731, tells the story in detail, more than a century later. Henry Chadwick discusses how much the Christians already in Britain and the Irish mission also contributed, and the page does not decide the question.

## Worship and community life

### Monks and the Rule

Benedict's Rule for monks sets prayer seven times in the day, as the Prophet says (Rule 16). It says idleness is the enemy of the soul, so the brethren should be occupied at certain times in manual labor and at fixed hours in sacred reading (Rule 48), and it calls obedience without delay the first degree of humility (Rule 5). Henry Chadwick describes how its influence grew slowly in the West.

### Bishops and the state

In the West, Gregory's editor describes him managing St. Peter's patrimony through agents and negotiating with the Lombard king. In the East, Justinian himself condemned the Three Chapters and called the council of 553 that condemned them.
