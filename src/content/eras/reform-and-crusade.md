## Overview

The sixth era ended with the mutual excommunications of 1054. This era runs from that break to the Fourth Lateran Council of 1215. In those years the papacy claimed a new authority over kings and bishops, western armies went on crusade to the East, the monastic and friar movements changed the church's work, and the church fixed its doctrine and the duties of its members in a single great council.

The page is arranged by East and West, as before. Events are tagged East, West or Both.

## Key debates

### Who should appoint bishops?

The Investiture Controversy was a long dispute between popes and emperors over whether kings could invest bishops with ring and staff. It ended in compromise in 1122, but the page does not yet assess how far either side won.

### Why did the crusades go where they did?

The First Crusade aimed at Jerusalem, but the Fourth turned against Constantinople. Historians debate the motives of the crusaders and the popes, and whether the diversion was planned. This page does not yet settle these questions.

### What was the role of the friars?

Franciscans and Dominicans preached in towns and cities, which was new for religious orders. Their significance for the church's pastoral work is debated, and the page does not yet give a view.

## Worship and community life

### Monasteries and the reform of religious life

The Cistercians, founded at Cîteaux in 1098, emphasised a simple life and manual work. Their houses spread quickly across Europe. Details not yet checked.

### Law and the schools

Gratian's collection of church law, around 1140, became a basis for the study of canon law in the universities. The details of the collection and its dating are not yet checked.
