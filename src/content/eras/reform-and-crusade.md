## Overview

The sixth era ended with the excommunication of 1054. This era runs from there to the Fourth Lateran Council of 1215. In those years popes and kings fought over who should appoint bishops, western armies went on crusade to the East, new orders of monks and then of friars spread across the West, and a great council defined the Eucharist and the duties of every Christian.

The page is arranged by East and West, as before. Events are tagged East, West or Both. The sources read for it are Western: medieval documents in translation, and three later histories that disagree on some dates. Where they disagree, the page gives both.

## Key debates

### Who should appoint bishops?

Gregory VII forbade kings to invest bishops, and Henry IV's bishops declared him deposed in 1076. Canossa did not end the quarrel. The Concordat of Worms in 1122 was a compromise: the church gave bishops the ring and crosier, and the emperor gave them their lands with the sceptre. Schaff and Ogg judge Canossa differently, as the event above shows.

### Why did the crusades go where they did?

The First Crusade was called to help the Christians of the East and to reach Jerusalem. The Fourth turned first to Zara, to pay the Venetians, and then to Constantinople, to restore a deposed emperor in return for his son's promises. Innocent III forbade both attacks and condemned the sack. The sources read here describe how the diversion happened but do not settle whether anyone planned it from the start.

### What was the role of the friars?

Francis and his companions lived in poverty and preached, and Innocent III approved their way of life in 1209 or 1210. Schaff treats the Franciscans and Dominicans together as the mendicant orders. This page does not go further into their work, which belongs mostly to the next era.

## Worship and community life

### Monasteries and the reform of religious life

Robert of Molesme settled with twenty companions near Cîteaux in 1098, and the Cistercians who followed him made manual labour an essential part of monastic life. Schaff says new houses followed rapidly and spread over all Western Europe. Bernard of Clairvaux was the order's most famous monk.

### Law and the schools

Gratian, a monk who taught at Bologna, compiled his collection of church law, the *Concordance of Discordant Canons* or *Decretum*, about 1140 to 1150. Schaff says it at once became the standard manual of canon law.
